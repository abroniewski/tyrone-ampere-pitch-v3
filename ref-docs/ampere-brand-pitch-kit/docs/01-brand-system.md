# 01. Brand System

## Core idea

The visual language combines industrial confidence with future-facing precision. It should feel engineered, not decorated.

## Five governing principles

### 1. Black creates gravity

Use near-black as the default environment for first impressions, key statements, portfolio indexes, and conversion moments. It creates the visual mass that makes cobalt feel energized.

### 2. Blue means action or acceleration

Electric cobalt should identify:

- Primary calls to action.
- Hover-state fills.
- Large statistics.
- Directional markers.
- Key words or data points.
- Major campaign fields.

Do not use it as a casual decorative tint across every component.

### 3. Typography carries the brand

The dominant device is not an illustration. It is a very large, thin headline with deliberate line breaks and tight tracking. Each line should feel placed, not automatically wrapped.

### 4. Geometry creates movement

Use oblique cuts, asymmetric masks, and acute polygons on major media. Avoid rounded rectangles, soft blobs, and ornamental curves.

### 5. Proof keeps it industrial

Every section should contain at least one concrete element:

- A number.
- A decision.
- A company.
- A role.
- A result.
- A direct action.

## Logo treatment

The public identity uses an angular symbol paired with a light wordmark. This package includes `concept-mark.svg`, which is an original placeholder and is not the official Ampere mark.

For production:

- Request the approved primary horizontal SVG.
- Request symbol-only and reversed variants.
- Do not redraw the official mark from a screenshot.
- Preserve the SVG viewBox and convert live text to outlines only in an approved master.
- Use white on black, black on white, or white on electric blue.
- Avoid gradients, shadows, outlines, or containers around the mark.

Recommended temporary clear space: one symbol width on every side. Replace with the official standard when supplied.

## Shape language

Use three families of shape:

1. **Hero wedge**: a large, asymmetric mask that enters from the right and visually cuts into the black field.
2. **Rising shard**: a cobalt-to-silver polygon used in posters, CTAs, and campaign moments.
3. **Triangle cue**: a small solid triangle used before links, labels, and navigation prompts.

The hero wedge should appear no more than once per page. The rising shard may recur in major section transitions. Triangle cues can repeat at small scale.

## Image treatment

Preferred photography:

- People in real operating environments.
- Electrical infrastructure and equipment.
- Manufacturing, fabrication, field work, and commissioning.
- Leadership shown in motion, not only studio portraits.
- Wide negative space that accommodates typography or a polygon crop.

Treatment:

- Cool the image toward blue.
- Increase contrast and deepen blacks.
- Use monochrome or near-monochrome for hero imagery.
- Preserve enough midtone detail for human faces and equipment.
- Layer subtle grids or line-work only when it adds structure.

Avoid:

- Generic stock images of handshakes.
- Neon cyberpunk effects.
- Overly glossy energy imagery.
- Heavy lens flares.
- Random blue gradients unrelated to content.

## Iconography

Icons should be:

- One-pixel or 1.5-pixel strokes.
- Square or angular.
- Used sparingly.
- Visually subordinate to type.

The preferred directional icon is a simple northeast arrow. The preferred micro-marker is a filled triangle.

## Texture and depth

Depth comes from:

- Overlapping black and blue fields.
- Image blur and scale.
- One-pixel grids.
- Controlled grain.
- Motion between layers.

Avoid card shadows and glassmorphism. The system should feel cut, printed, machined, and illuminated.
