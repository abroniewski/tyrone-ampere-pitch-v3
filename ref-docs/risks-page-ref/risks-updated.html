Step 3 · The cost of the status quo

Nothing on this page is invented.

Every risk below is something you said on the call, taken exactly one step forward using your own numbers.

1

The speed story expires on schedule

"We're about to fill our entire pipeline for the next year. So we won't be fast soon. And then everyone's going to forget about us for a year."

The current differentiator has a known expiration date. Once the pipeline fills, Ampere enters a positioning gap: "fast" is no longer true, but the replacement value proposition has not yet been built.

12 MONTHS
The period in which the current sales story stops working, according to your own forecast.
2

You hire a US sales team with nothing to hand them

No value propositions. No credible decks. No account knowledge base. No onboarding material. Every month the team remains dependent on agents is another month the old commercial model keeps running.

$500K TO $1M PER $10M
The agent economics attached to every $10M of revenue the internal team is not yet ready to handle. That is $500k at the normal 5% rate and $1M at the exceptional 10% arrangement.
3

The agent math keeps compounding

The standard agent arrangement is 5% of revenue. At a 30% gross margin, that consumes 16.7% of the gross profit on every agent-led sale.

On the potential $120M project, the agent is demanding 10%, equal to $12M. Another $200M has been quoted, bringing the stated agent economics across the two projects to as much as $32M.

$16M AT THE STANDARD RATE
Five percent of the $320M in opportunities you described.
UP TO $32M AT THE EXCEPTIONAL RATE
The amount you said the agent could make if the 10% arrangement applies across both projects.

The internal team does not need to be cheap. It only needs to cost less than that.

4

Perception is compounding against you

"Perception is everything. You need to look like an established company. Otherwise why the hell am I going to trust you to take on this multi-million-dollar project?"

Ampere is presenting to buyers considering projects worth hundreds of millions. At that scale, the materials do not need to lose an entire deal to become expensive. They only need to introduce a little doubt at the wrong moment.

1 POINT ACROSS $320M = $960K
Across the two opportunities, one percentage point of win probability represents $3.2M in expected revenue and $960k in expected gross profit at a 30% margin.
Deal-value sensitivity, not a claim that a presentation creates exactly one percentage point.
5

You become the bottleneck of your own plan

You said you should be building strategic relationships, meeting customers, designing value propositions, helping close major accounts, and shaping the product pipeline.

Instead, you have already spent roughly two weeks troubleshooting websites, presentation layouts, recoloured images, and an AI design system that made the output worse.

TWO WEEKS ALREADY SPENT
Before the wider group marketing system has even been built.

On the $120M opportunity, a one-percentage-point improvement in win probability represents $360k in expected gross profit. The question is not what two weeks of your salary cost. It is what those two weeks could have done on the deal.

The risk isn't that the marketing stays bad. It's that YOU stay the only thing standing between bad and good.

Every month that remains true keeps the agents expensive, the internal team dependent, and you out of the rooms where fractions of a percentage point are worth hundreds of thousands of dollars.
