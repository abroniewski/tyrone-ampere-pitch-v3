Step 2 · The problem

It's not a sales problem.

Demand isn't your issue. Data center customers are begging you to build more. You said it yourself: selling to the US market is easy right now.

So why does everything feel stuck?

You don't have a sales problem. You have an asset problem: every valuable thing said in your business EVAPORATES the moment the conversation ends.

Five symptoms, in your own words

Value propositions
"We build things. We've built things before. We can build things for you."

That's the current pitch across the group. You're building value props from scratch, with a new president, while everyone yells "get the sales team going."

Assets
"We look like we just crawled out of your basement."

Websites you can't send to a customer. The worst decks you've ever seen, in front of buyers deciding on multi-million dollar projects.

AI workflow
"I've spent like 2 weeks on a lot of this."

A design system that made output worse than none. Hours of finagling per deck. A Gemini website that claimed to exist and didn't.

People
"There's just nobody I trust that can do it to my standard."

Internal marketing you can't rely on (the $7,000 brother-website episode). Freelancers you don't trust. No time to coach anyone.

Your calendar
"What I should be doing is strategic relationships... help close big customers."

Instead, the person responsible for $500M is recoloring images in PowerPoint and troubleshooting white space.

These look like five problems. They're one.

Nothing you learn, say, or make is captured in a form you can reuse. Every deck starts from zero. Every value prop lives in your head. Every asset is a hyper-specific one-off you'll never use again.

And every hire has to be you, because you're the only database the company has. That's why the template experiment with your reps failed... you handed them a design system when what they were missing was the knowledge underneath it.
