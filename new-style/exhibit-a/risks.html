Step 3 · The cost of the status quo

Nothing on this page is invented.

Every risk below is something you said on the call, taken exactly one step forward.

1

The speed story expires on schedule

"We're about to fill our entire pipeline for the next year. So we won't be fast soon. And then everyone's going to forget about us for a year."

A year of being forgotten, with no value proposition to replace "fast," while you're trying to grow toward a billion. The replacement story has to exist before the pipeline fills, not after.

2

You hire a US sales team with nothing to hand them

No value props, no decks worth sending, no onboarding material. I've lived the receiving end of this: "go make sales" with nothing to send is hell, and it burns good people. Every month the new team ramps slowly is a month the old costs keep running.

3

The agent math keeps compounding

5% of revenue is about 17% of your gross margin at a 30% margin. One agent is lined up to make $32M across two projects. The internal team that replaces that needs assets from day one, or the transition stalls and the commissions keep flowing.

4

Perception is compounding against you

"Perception is everything. You need to look like an established company. Otherwise why the hell am I going to trust you to take on this multi-million dollar project?"

Your words, again. Right now the group's materials are answering that question the wrong way, at every lunch-and-learn, in front of exactly the buyers you need.

5

You become the bottleneck of your own plan

Strategy, presidents, M&A, mega deals... that's the job. Every hour spent on white space in slide four is an hour the $500M plan doesn't get. And you told me you struggle with the last mile of projects, which means those hours multiply right where it hurts most.

The risk isn't that the marketing stays bad. It's that YOU stay the only thing standing between bad and good.
