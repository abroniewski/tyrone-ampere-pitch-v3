Stage 1 · Signals

Find companies with a reason to act, not names on a list.

Lead generation

A new data center permit is a live pain signal. I build semi-automated workflows that watch the signals that matter for Ampere: data center construction permits, utility interconnection queues, grid modernization programs, electrification projects, plant expansions. Anything that says someone will soon need switchgear, transformers, energy storage, or the partner who can deliver all of it.

The workflow collects the companies behind each signal, separates real buyers from noise, and hands a person a reviewed list. It removes the hunting and sorting. It does not decide who deserves outreach, and it never contacts anyone on its own.

Stage 2 · Enrichment

Turn each signal into a useful account and person-level view.

Context → CRM

This happens to every lead list, regardless of which signal created it. Each company is enriched with headcount, turnover, sector, the signal that surfaced it, and a scored view of how strongly it fits the ideal customer profile.

From that account set, I use tools like Sales Navigator to find the people who best match the target persona, Clay to enrich each person with additional context from their LinkedIn presence and company, and Apollo and similar tools to add verified email addresses and phone numbers for outreach.

The CRM record keeps both layers: why the company surfaced and why this person is the right route in. The same structure then generates a grounded personal opener without losing the evidence behind it.

What every enriched record carries

Company fit: scale, turnover, sector, triggering signal, and ICP score.

Person fit: role, seniority, likely proximity to the pain, and the most credible route into the account.

Outreach context: the precise reason this company and this person are worth contacting now.

Stage 3 · Outreach

Turn enriched context into a human-led multi-touch workflow.

LinkedIn, email, phone

From each enriched list, I create the full outreach workflow: individually personalized LinkedIn interactions, email touches, and cold-call scripts. Every channel starts from the same company signal, person-level context, and likely pain.

The information lives in the CRM and supports semi-automated execution. I deliberately stop short of full automation. During early go-to-market work and product-market-fit discovery, replies, objections, role corrections, silence, and language choices are valuable signals. Full automation can optimize away the learning.

The system carries the research and consistency. The salesperson still carries the relationship and decides what the next touch should be.

Custom enough to feel personal

Part of the sequence is a use-case asset that reads like a finished corporate document. Underneath, it is assembled from reusable approved blocks plus sections built specifically for that person, that company, and the pain they are most likely feeling.

Stage 4 · The engine room

Conversations become permanent, reusable commercial knowledge.

The compounding core

Every meeting is recorded and dropped into one folder. A nightly run turns the transcript into structured notes, the notes into an account dossier, and the dossier into reusable proof points, objections, stakeholder maps, economics, and value propositions. A pre-call brief pulls that history back out before the next meeting.

Stage 5 · Sales assets

Use the next asset to remove the exact reason the deal is stuck.

Demos and proposals

After a meeting, the transcript can generate the follow-up email, the story for an asynchronous demo, and the supporting proposal or value case. The asset is built around the objection that actually appeared in the conversation, not around a generic product tour.

In this example, I recorded a Loom demo after the meeting and sent it in a transcript-grounded follow-up email. The demonstration ran through the product's capabilities using the prospect's own data.

They had already said no because they believed their unique custom environment would be too complicated and our software could not handle it. The follow-up changed the decision and opened a proof-of-value engagement.

A no became a proof of value

Stage 6 · Marketing and learning

Turn commercial knowledge into company visibility, personal reach, and better next bets.

Marketing engine

This stage is broader than company content. The engine room supplies real pains, language, objections, and proof. From the same knowledge base, the system produces company assets such as use-case pages, newsletters, infographics, and carousels, as well as personal posts that build visibility, credibility, and networking opportunities.

CRM engagement and website analytics create an evidence trail for which messages, pages, and posts are working. Sales conversations supply the raw material. Performance data tells us what to develop next and which signal should start the next trip around the loop.

The rails beneath every stage

Design system

The brand is code, not a paragraph

CSS tokens for colors and type. Reusable HTML patterns. Rule files the agent must load before producing anything visual. Repeatable rendering and export. The constraints live in the files, so the output cannot casually drift. This is why your prose-instruction design system made things worse and this one doesn't.

Guardrails

Every claim keeps its receipts

Stats and quotes are tagged by provenance and publication status. Pre-send checks block unsupported claims and sensitive material. Feedback becomes a permanent rule, so the same correction does not return wearing a new hat. And every proof point carries its ICP era, because I learned in a pivot that old evidence can poison a new story.

The practical difference

Today, when something changes

Strategy changes → you remember which assets exist → people search folders → several files get missed → reps keep old copies → the next change starts the scavenger hunt again.

With the living system

Strategy changes → source objects update → affected assets appear in a review queue → the right owners approve → updated views publish → sales receives the release → old versions retire.

This pitch already ran that workflow manually. One strategic insight changed the source model, which forced updates to the extraction, the inventory, the mapping, the narrative, and the site you're reading. The Ampere build turns that discipline into an operating system.
