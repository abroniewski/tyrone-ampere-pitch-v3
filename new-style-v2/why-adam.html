What the pivot taught me

Concretely

Two things got me through the pivot. I built a system that turns every recorded conversation into reusable commercial knowledge: account dossiers, proof points, objections, quotes. And I drafted an anchor value proposition: a versioned document that every new call either confirmed or corrected, so it got stronger every week instead of going stale.

The pivot taught me why both need to be versioned. The old ICP's proof points didn't just become useless. They became DANGEROUS: quoting the wrong era's evidence to the new buyer undermines the new story. So the system tags every proof point with its era, and old-era claims are filtered out before reuse. That's not a feature you think of until a pivot burns you.

The anchor value proposition proved the same point: version one was wrong in places, and that was fine, because it existed, it was versioned, and the feedback loop kept correcting it. You don't need the right answer to start. You need a system that makes the answer converge.

And yes: my Konekti contract wasn't renewed, because the pivot's traction hasn't turned into recurring revenue fast enough to keep me. I'm telling you that plainly for two reasons. You should hear it from me first. And it's why the timing of this conversation is almost stupid.

I'm not a content producer who needs a finished strategy handed to me. I build the commercial scaffolding while the strategy is still becoming clear.
