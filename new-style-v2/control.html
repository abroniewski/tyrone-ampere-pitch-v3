The Living GTM System · Private pitch

(04) Control

One front door. Many owners.

The tension

Design requirement

The presidents own their P&Ls. That's not an obstacle. It's a design requirement, and any commercial system that ignores it is dead on arrival.

The presidents carry real accountability: their results, their technical claims, their customer relationships. A system that asks them to give that up deserves to fail.

You already saw the shape of the answer yourself: one unified Ampere front, with customers and opportunities routed to the right company under the hood. The system just needs to make that instinct operational.

The future system has to respect a tension you described precisely: the market should see one professional Ampere, the operating companies stay legally and commercially distinct, the presidents own their P&Ls and want control... and you still need consistency, routing, and group-level advantage.

The design principle

Centralize the truth, not every decision

(01)

Ampere group owns

Owns 5
Challenge

The market needs one professional Ampere story, not five disconnected company fronts.

Solution
  • The group narrative and master brand standards
  • The portfolio capability map
  • Shared ICP and solution definitions
  • Common proof and claim rules
  • The unified customer entry point
Result

Group coherence without forcing every company into identical local decisions.

(02)

Presidents own or approve

Owns 5
Challenge

Presidents carry real accountability: the P&L, the technical claims, the customer relationships. Those stay theirs.

Solution
  • Company-specific technical claims
  • Local proof and references
  • Capacity and delivery constraints
  • Vertical-specific nuance
  • Company-level commercial exceptions
Result

Local control stays real while still feeding a coherent group front.

(03)

You own

Owns 5
Challenge

Someone has to hold cross-company GTM architecture and resolve conflicts without becoming the permanent bottleneck.

Solution
  • Cross-company GTM architecture
  • Final group coherence
  • Priority and release decisions
  • Conflict resolution
  • The sales enablement standard
Result

You stay on the CRO work: deals, presidents, acquisitions. Not last-mile production.

(04)

The system owns

Owns 5
Challenge

Without machinery, truth drifts: stale versions keep selling, and nobody knows what a change touched.

Solution
  • Traceability and versioning
  • Impact lists when something changes
  • Routing and status
  • Deprecation of stale versions
  • Notification, so nobody sells old truth
Result

Federated approval becomes operational instead of tribal knowledge.

Presidents keep their focus where it creates the most value: their customers, their technology, their results. The system makes that local control compatible with one coherent Ampere front... and hands each company stronger assets and bigger opportunities in return.

And it has to work FOR the presidents, not around them. Each one gets stronger assets, more qualified leads, and access to larger multi-company projects, while keeping approval over every claim made about their company. If the system doesn't create visible local value, it doesn't deserve adoption.

Without that system, the strategy creates predictable risks
